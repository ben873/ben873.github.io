ATTENDED MIXING

Do I Need an Attended Sound Mix for My Feature?

What an Attended Mix Actually Is

An attended mix doesn’t usually mean that the director and producers sit behind the mixer while the soundtrack is built from scratch.

By the time the creative team arrives, the production should generally be fully mixed from a technical standpoint. Dialogue has been balanced, backgrounds and effects have been integrated, music has been placed, automation has been written and the overall dynamics of the production have been established.

The purpose of the attended session is primarily creative.

It gives the director and other members of the creative team an opportunity to hear the finished mix in a calibrated monitoring environment designed to approximate the experience of a small theater. They can experience the soundtrack at an appropriate playback level, with proper speaker placement and bass management, rather than trying to judge a theatrical mix from a laptop, television or unfamiliar home system.

From there, the conversation becomes about creative choices.

A director may decide that the score should carry a particular moment instead of the effects. An ambience might feel too present once it’s heard at theatrical level. A transition might benefit from becoming dramatically quieter. A specific effect may deserve more attention, or a line that seemed perfectly clear during editorial may need a little more presence in the context of the completed mix.

These aren’t necessarily corrections to an unfinished mix. They’re creative decisions made after hearing the production as an audience is intended to hear it.

That’s the real value of an attended mix: the production arrives mixed, and the creative team gets to make final creative decisions from a reliable theatrical reference environment.

For an independent production, the decision to book an attended mix should therefore be based less on whether it’s necessary to finish the soundtrack and more on whether the production values having that final creative decision-making process take place together in a theatrical monitoring environment.
